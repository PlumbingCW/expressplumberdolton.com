Hydro jetting in Dolton, IL is a drain cleaning method that pushes water at very high pressure through a hose with a nozzle, scouring grease, scale and root hair off the inside of a sewer or drain pipe. Backup happening now: what to do while you wait

Jetting is not a first-aid step you can do yourself. These moves limit the damage until a technician arrives.

Stop every water source

Turn off the washing machine, dishwasher and shower. A line that is already full cannot take another load.

Lift what you can off the floor

Move boxes, stored clothing and cardboard away from the basement drain. Wet cardboard soaks up sewage and is hard to save.

Leave pressure washers alone

A rented washer pushed into a drain does not clear a main line. It can force dirty water back into the house.

Photograph the damage

Take pictures of the water line and affected items before you clean. Your insurance agent will ask for them.

Jetting versus cabling: the honest comparison

A cable cuts a hole through a clog. A jetter washes the pipe wall clean. Neither is better in every case, so here is how they differ.

Where a cable wins

  • Quick to set up at 2 a.m. for a full blockage
  • Good on a single toilet or sink branch
  • Gentler on a pipe we have not seen yet
  • Lower cost when the clog is a one-time event

Where jetting wins

  • Removes the grease coating a cable leaves behind
  • Cuts and flushes root hair at the joint
  • Clears a 4 inch main line wall to wall
  • Slows the cycle of repeat backups

If a cable has already been through the same line twice this year, the next step is usually a camera inspection, not another pass with the same tool.

What scoring does to roots and grease

Jetting is cleaning, not magic. This is what the water actually does inside the pipe.

Grease

A rotating nozzle throws water against the wall and strips the greasy film that builds up past a kitchen tee. The fat is flushed to the main instead of smeared.

Root hair

Fine roots growing into a joint are cut back and carried out. The root outside the pipe stays alive and can grow back, often in a year or two.

Scale

Mineral buildup in an older iron line can be cleaned off in light layers. Thick rust ridges may need a mechanical cutter first.

Soap and wipe masses

Wads of wipes and soap scum break up under pressure and wash downstream, where the larger line can carry them.

Silt and sand

Debris that settles in a low spot is pushed along to a point where we can recover or flush it out.

When jetting is the wrong call

A good plumber turns down jobs that a jetter would make worse. We run a camera first when any of these apply.

  • The pipe is old clay or Orangeburg that may be cracked or collapsed
  • A cable stopped at the same distance every time, which points to a break or offset
  • A joint is already leaking into the soil, since pressure can widen the gap
  • The drain is a thin plastic line under a slab or an unknown material
  • The house has had water damage that traces back to a failing line

How a jetting visit runs from start to finish

The order matters, and skipping the first step is how a fragile pipe gets damaged.

1

Camera first

We feed a small camera down through the cleanout so we see the material, the condition and what is clogging it.

2

Choose the nozzle

Forward jets pull the hose through, rear jets scour the wall, and a cutting nozzle handles roots.

3

Work in passes

The hose goes in at moderate pressure, then we pull back slowly so the debris travels toward the main.

4

Camera again

We look at the clean pipe and show you the footage. If the pipe is damaged, we explain the repair options and the price before any work.
