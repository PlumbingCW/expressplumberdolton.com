Water line repair in Dolton, IL is work on the underground pipe that carries water from the village main and meter to your house. Our 24/7 emergency line is answered nights and weekends, and if the line has broken, close the main valve inside the house and call us before the yard turns to mud.

This page walks through the line itself, who owns which part, the signs it is leaking, how a repair differs from a replacement, and what changes when we can avoid a trench.

Where your water line starts and who looks after what

Water reaches your house through a service line that connects to the main under the street. Near the property there is a shutoff and a meter, and from there the pipe runs to a point where it enters the building. The part past the meter is generally the owner's concern, but the arrangement can differ by property, so we ask before we dig.

The village side of the line is a separate matter. Dolton Public Works handles water mains and meters at 708-201-3280, and the Water Department takes meter questions at 708-201-3293. After hours, the village directs callers to its Dispatch Center at 708-201-3240. Calling the village early helps, because a leak on its side is not yours to repair. We also handle line breaks for neighbors in South Holland.

Water line leaking now: what to do while you wait

A broken line can empty into the yard or a basement wall. Take these steps first.

Shut the main inside the house

A wheel valve closes clockwise. A lever valve closes with a quarter turn across the pipe.

Do not run water

No showers, no dishwasher, no washing machine. Open one low faucet to bleed the remaining pressure.

Keep people off soft ground

Soaked soil can collapse near a break. Mark the spot and keep children and pets away.

Do not dig

Illinois law requires calling JULIE at 811 at least 48 hours before digging, and the call is free. A shovel through an unmarked line is a larger problem.

Signs the water line is the problem

The common signs are about the whole house, not one fixture.

Wet or sunken yard

A patch that stays soggy in dry weather, or turf that dips between the meter and the house.

Meter spinning with everything off

Close the interior main and watch again. If it still moves, water is escaping on the buried section.

Low pressure everywhere

When every tap runs weak, the line, a partly closed valve or a regulator is more likely than a single fixture.

Dirty or discolored water

A broken or corroded line can let sediment in. Run a cold tap for a minute and report what you see.

Basement wall damp where the line enters

Water can follow the pipe sleeve into the foundation, and then appears inside as a stain.

Repair the line or replace it

The honest answer comes from where the break is and what the pipe looks like.

Spot repair

  • Cuts a small section and splices in new pipe
  • Suits one clean break in otherwise sound pipe
  • Less excavation, faster water restoration
  • May leave old pipe on either side

Full line replacement

  • Replaces pipe from the meter area to the house
  • Makes sense for old, pitted or repeatedly leaking lines
  • Can allow a larger line or a new material
  • More cost and more surface disturbance

How a water line repair goes

The process is the same whether we use a trench or not.

1

Confirm the leak

A meter test, then leak detection gear to find the break, so we dig once and in the right place.

2

Mark and plan

JULIE 811 marks buried utilities, which takes at least 48 hours. We plan the route around marked lines, trees and paving.

3

Open the ground or pull a new line

Either a trench to the break, or a trenchless method where a new pipe is pulled through a small pit at each end.

4

Test and close up

We pressure test the new section, check the meter, and backfill. Landscape and paving repair are separate trades.

Lead service lines and what the village inventory means for you

Dolton's water report says lead in drinking water comes mainly from service lines and home plumbing, and that the village has developed a service line material inventory. The report does not publish a count, and it does not say what your own line is made of. The 2025 report shows a lead 90th percentile of 10.7 ppb against an action level of 15 ppb, with 3 sampled sites above it.

Here is the practical tie-back. Before a repair, ask the Dolton Water Department at 708-201-3293 or Public Works at 708-201-3280 what material your service line is. If it is lead or galvanized, tell us before we plan the work, since the replacement choice changes. We will not guess the material from the street.

What this work does not cover

We do not repair the village main, and if the leak is on the village side of the meter we will tell you to call Public Works. We also do not repair lawns or concrete after an excavation, though we leave the area safe. If a leak turns out to be inside the house, our burst pipe repair page covers that, and if the problem is low pressure with no leak, repiping may be the answer.

Digging rules and who to call about the village main

Illinois law requires calling JULIE at 811 at least 48 hours before digging so utility lines are marked, free of charge, according to Nicor Gas customer guidance. A water line repair involves digging, so that call comes first, whoever does the work.

The village side of the connection belongs to Dolton Public Works at 708-201-3280. Call them if the problem could be the main or the meter, and call us when the evidence points to your own service line.

Sources: JULIE (811) per Nicor Gas guidance, Village of Dolton Public Works.
