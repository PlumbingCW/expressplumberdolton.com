Burst pipe repair in Dolton, IL means stopping the flow from a split, cracked or separated pipe and replacing the damaged section so the line holds pressure again. Our 24/7 emergency line is answered at night and on weekends, and while you wait you should close the main valve and keep power away from the water.

Everything below is in the order you will need it. Shutoff steps first, then what caused the burst, then repair options by pipe material, and then what we do and do not handle after the water is gone.

Stop the water in four moves

Do these while you wait. Most of the damage in a burst comes from the minutes before anyone closes a valve.

1. Close the main

Find the shutoff where the water line enters the house. Turn a wheel handle clockwise, or turn a lever a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe. If it will not move, do not force it with a wrench, call us.

2. Drain the lines

Open the lowest faucet and the highest one. Flush one toilet. This empties the pipes so the broken section stops leaking.

3. Kill power near the water

If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, do not stand in it. Switch off the affected circuit only from a dry spot. If the panel itself is wet, leave and call 911 or the utility.

4. Photograph, then contain

Take dated photos before you move anything, then use towels, buckets and a wet vacuum for what you can reach. Move electronics and boxes up off the floor.

Freeze burst or failure burst

Pipes burst for two broad reasons, and the cause changes the repair. Water expands when it freezes, and the pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet until the pipe splits, often along a seam. That damage usually shows up when the ice melts, so a quiet pipe can become a flood in the afternoon.

A failure burst has no freeze behind it. Old galvanized steel corrodes from the inside, copper pinholes through, a fitting separates, or a pressure spike hammers a weak spot. These bursts usually mean more of the pipe is weak, so we look beyond the break.

Dolton's hazard plan ranks severe winter weather second among local hazards, which makes a cold-snap burst a practical risk. If you see frost on an exposed pipe in a garage or a basement wall, that is your cue to open a tap to a trickle and call us before it splits.

Repair the section or replace more pipe

A tradesperson's honest answer depends on what the rest of the pipe looks like.

Cut out and patch the burst

  • Fastest way to restore water the same visit when parts are available
  • Works when the surrounding pipe is sound
  • Right for a freeze split in good copper or PEX
  • Leaves older sections as they were

Replace a longer run

  • Better when pipe is pitted, scaled or has leaked before
  • More wall access, more time
  • Avoids repeat bursts in the same line
  • Often the honest call for galvanized steel

If one burst has exposed a pattern of failing pipe, our repiping page explains when the whole system is worth replacing.

Repair options by pipe material

The material you have decides the fitting, the tools and how much wall is opened.

Copper pipe repair

We cut out the split, then join new copper with soldered fittings or press fittings. Copper that has pinholed in several places is a replacement candidate.

PEX

Flexible and tolerant of freezing, PEX can often be cut and joined with crimp or expansion fittings. Not every PEX split is freeze damage, so we check the fittings too.

Galvanized steel

Threaded and corroded inside, it often breaks again beside the repair. We assess the length before agreeing a patch is worth it.

PVC and CPVC

Cut out and re-glued with a coupling. Rigid plastic can be brittle in cold, so a cracked line may need a longer section.

Burst supply valve

A cracked stop valve or a failed shutoff under a sink or behind a toilet is replaced with a new valve on the supply line.

Drain pipe

A cracked or separated drain is repaired with a new section and coupling. Check for a sewer smell, which points to a waste line, not supply.

After the repair: drying, drywall and insurance photos

We repair pipes. We do not do water damage restoration, so drying wet walls, pulling soaked insulation and rebuilding drywall go to a restoration company. If water has been in a wall for more than a day or two, tell them, since damp wood and insulation need active drying.

The Illinois Department of Insurance advises photographing damage and keeping an inventory of what was affected, and its consumer hotline is 1-866-445-5364. Its point on sewer backup and flood coverage is that basic homeowners policies do not cover floods, and standard flood coverage does not typically cover sewer backup, so a burst supply line is a separate question to ask your agent. Keep any old pipe we remove if your insurer asks to see it.

What to tell the dispatcher

A few facts help us send the right parts on the first visit.

  • Whether the water is shut off at the main yet
  • Where the pipe is: ceiling, wall, basement, garage or outside
  • The pipe material if you can tell, copper color or flexible plastic
  • Whether the burst follows a freeze or a pressure surge
  • Whether any electrical outlet or panel is wet
  • Whether you can shut off the valve nearest the leak

When the repair is a bigger job than it looks

A split pipe in plain view is a quick job. A burst inside a finished wall or buried under a slab is not, because the wall has to be opened to reach it. We try to keep access holes small and tell you before cutting. If the pipe is a drain, the repair may involve a camera, and our sewer line repair page covers breaks outside the house.

We also look for the cause of a failure. A pressure spike from a faulty pressure reducing valve can burst a healthy pipe, and a frozen exterior hose bib can split the line behind it. Fixing the break without fixing the cause invites a second call.
