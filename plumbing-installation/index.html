Plumbing installation in Dolton, IL covers adding or replacing the pipes, valves and fixtures that make a space work, from a new kitchen faucet to rough-in for a basement bathroom. The 24/7 line is answered, and same-day appointments are offered when available, but installs are best planned ahead.

We are licensed and insured, and we quote the work before it starts. Because installs touch walls, floors and sometimes the village sewer connection, the first step is a conversation about what you want and what the house allows.

What we install

These are the most common requests.

Fixtures

Sinks, faucets, toilets, tubs, showers and laundry boxes, with new supply lines and stops.

Remodel rough-in

Supply and drain lines placed behind open walls before the finish work begins, so the new layout works.

Relocations

Moving a sink, toilet or washer to a new wall means new supply and a new drain path with the right slope.

Shutoff valves

Whole-house and fixture valves that actually close when you need them. Old ones that stick get replaced.

Appliance hookups

Dishwasher, ice maker and washing machine connections, with a proper drain and shutoff.

Softeners and filters

Water softener and filter hookups with a bypass valve and a drain connection.

How an install job goes

Planned work follows this order.

1

Talk and look

We see the space, review your plan or fixture and flag problems with venting, drain slope or space before pricing.

2

Price and schedule

3

Rough-in and test

Pipe goes in, then a pressure test on supply lines and a water test on drains before walls close.

4

Set and finish

Fixtures are set, connections tightened, and every valve and drain gets checked running.

Replace a fixture or move it

Swapping a fixture in place is simpler than relocating it, and the difference in work is large.

Replace in place

  • Uses existing supply and drain locations
  • Often a single visit
  • Fewer walls opened
  • Limited by the old connections

Relocate or add

  • Needs new supply, drain and vent routing
  • Floor or wall access is required
  • May affect nearby framing
  • Opens up a new layout

For a stuck fixture rather than a new one, our faucet repair or toilet repair pages may be a better fit.

Remodels and new construction rough-in

Rough-in is the plumbing that goes into open walls and floors before drywall. It sets where every drain, supply and vent will sit, and mistakes are expensive to fix once the walls are closed. We work from your plan, check fixture clearances and test before any finish work starts.

For a new build or an addition, the sequence matters. Underground drains come first, then supply and vent in the framing, then the pressure test, then the inspection that your village requires. We do not state what Dolton requires, so confirm permit and inspection rules with the building department at 708-201-3261. We also serve Harvey projects.

Water softener and dishwasher hookups

A water softener needs a cold supply, a bypass valve, a drain connection and an outlet nearby. Dolton does not report water hardness in its public report, so test your tap or ask your water utility before deciding the unit is needed. Our water softener and filtration page covers what these systems can and cannot do.

A dishwasher needs a hot supply with a stop, a drain connection with a high loop or an air gap, and a power connection. We handle the plumbing side. Electrical work belongs to an electrician.

Backwater valves and the sewer backup problem

The Cook County hazard mitigation plan for Dolton describes repeated basement flooding in several named areas and proposes retrofitting about 500 homes with overhead sewer systems, aimed at places where flooding happens when the sewer backs up in severe rain. The plan proposes this. It does not say it has happened, and it does not apply to every house.

The practical tie-back is simple. A backwater valve is worth asking about if your basement drain has backed up in storms. It sits in the building drain and closes when flow reverses. It does not stop a failed sump pump or groundwater seepage.

Ask your insurance agent about sewer backup coverage too, since the Illinois Department of Insurance says standard flood policies generally do not cover it. Our sump pump repair page covers the pump side.

Source: Cook County Hazard Mitigation Plan annex for Dolton, 2024 edition.

What we do not do on an install

We do not do gas line work unless your job calls for it and we say so ahead of time. If you smell natural gas, leave the building and call Nicor Gas at 888-642-6748 or 911 before calling us. We also do not do electrical, tile or cabinet work, and we do not give a final price over the phone without seeing the job.

What to do before an installation visit

A short checklist saves a second trip and keeps the job safe. These are the things we ask you to settle before a fixture or hookup goes in.

  • Clear the work area, including the cabinet under the sink or the wall around the new fixture
  • Find the main shutoff and any fixture shutoffs so water can be stopped quickly
  • Have the fixture and its installation sheet on site, not just the box
  • Ask the Dolton Housing and Permits office whether the job needs a permit, since rules vary
  • Do not run gas or electrical connections yourself, because those need their own licensed trades
