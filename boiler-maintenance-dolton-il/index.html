Boiler Maintenance and Repair in Dolton, IL

A boiler that gets a yearly check usually fails slowly and loudly, not suddenly in January. This guide covers what you can watch, what only a pro should touch and the carbon monoxide basics.

A boiler heats water, then sends that hot water or the steam it makes through pipes to radiators, baseboard units or in-floor loops. Boiler maintenance and repair in Dolton, IL is mostly about three things: the burner is clean, the water side is sound and the exhaust leaves the house. Cook County winters are cold and severe winter weather ranks second among the village's listed hazards, so a failed boiler in January is not an inconvenience.

You can do some of the checking yourself. Gas burners, controls and vents are work for a licensed plumber or heating contractor, and this post marks that line clearly.

What an owner can safely check each fall

Do these before the first cold night. None of them opens the burner or the gas train.

  • Look at the pressure gauge. Most home boilers sit around 12 to 15 psi when cold, but read your manual because models differ
  • Walk the pipes and look for drips, rust stains or crust at valves and fittings
  • Bleed air from radiators that stay cool at the top, following the manual
  • Clear storage, paint cans and laundry from around the boiler so air can reach it
  • Press the test button on the carbon monoxide alarm and replace dead batteries
  • Run the heat for ten minutes and listen for banging, whistling or a kettle-like boil

Carbon monoxide and boilers

Any boiler that burns gas or oil produces carbon monoxide if combustion is incomplete or the vent is blocked. The gas is odorless, so the alarm is your only warning. Headache, nausea or dizziness that clears when you leave the house is a clue.

If your alarm sounds, go outside and call 911. Do not open windows and stay inside to troubleshoot. If you smell natural gas, leave and call Nicor Gas at 888-642-6748 before you call a plumber. Soot around the boiler, a yellow or lazy flame and a pilot that keeps blowing out are signs for a technician, not for a screwdriver.

Common boiler faults and what they usually mean

Symptoms are clues, not diagnoses. These are the ones we hear about most.

Banging or kettling

Mineral buildup or low water flow inside the heat exchanger can make it boil in spots. Left alone, it shortens the boiler's life.

Pressure drops again and again

A pinhole leak, a failing expansion tank or a weeping relief valve is the likely cause. Refilling the gauge each week hides the problem and adds fresh oxygen to the system.

Radiators hot at the bottom, cold on top

Trapped air, so bleed the radiator. If one room stays cold after bleeding, a zone valve or circulator pump may be stuck.

No hot water from a combi or indirect tank

The diverter valve, the sensor or the tank thermostat is often to blame. This needs testing, not guessing.

Boiler locks out and shows a fault code

Resetting once is reasonable. If it locks out again, the control is protecting you from something, and repeated resets are a bad idea.

What a professional tune-up includes

Ask any plumber or heating company to walk you through their checklist. A good visit looks like this.

1

Inspect the exterior and the vent

The technician checks the flue pipe, the draft, the joints and the surrounding air supply for blockage or corrosion.

2

Clean the burner and test combustion

With the burner clean, a combustion analyzer reads oxygen and carbon monoxide in the flue gas and shows whether the flame is burning cleanly.

3

Test the safety controls

The low-water cutoff, the high-limit, the pressure relief valve and the flame sensor are each tested, since these are the parts that protect your home.

4

Review water side and system pressure

Pressure, expansion tank condition and the water itself are checked, and any leak or corrosion is explained with options.

Repair the boiler or replace it

There is no magic age. What matters is condition, parts and efficiency.

Repair makes sense when

  • The failure is a single part, such as a pump or a sensor
  • The heat exchanger and tank are sound
  • Parts are still available for the model
  • The boiler has run reliably until now

Replacement makes sense when

  • The heat exchanger is cracked or leaking
  • Repairs are frequent and keep climbing
  • The unit is hard to repair because parts are gone
  • Your home's heat demand has changed

Where the maintenance guidelines come from

The most reliable guideline for your boiler is the manufacturer's manual, because it lists the service interval, the water pressure range and the clearances for that exact model. A general checklist is a starting point, and the manual wins when the two disagree.

For anything involving gas, Nicor Gas guidance is blunt. If you smell gas, do not look for the source. Leave, then call 888-642-6748 or 911. A carbon monoxide alarm sounding is the same kind of event, so go outside first and make calls second.

Sources: manufacturer service manuals, Nicor Gas customer guidance.
