Emergency faucet repair in Dolton, IL means stopping water that will not shut off, a supply line spraying under a sink, or a tub or shower valve leaking into the wall. Our 24/7 line is answered nights and weekends. Under most sinks, two small stops control hot and cold, and a quarter turn clockwise closes each one. If a stop will not hold, go to the main water shutoff and call us.

A faucet that drips once a minute can wait for a daytime visit. A valve that will not close, a line that is spraying, or damp drywall behind a shower is different. We repair sink faucets, bath and shower valves, tub spouts and the shutoff stops themselves.

Water will not shut off: stop it before it spreads

Work in this order. Each step is a smaller version of the one before it.

Try the stops under the fixture

Turn each oval or round handle clockwise. If the water slows but does not stop, the stop valve itself is worn and needs replacing.

Close the main shutoff

It is usually where the water line enters the house, often in the basement near the meter. Know where it is before you need it.

Open a low tap to drain pressure

With the main closed, open the lowest faucet in the house. Line pressure drops and a spraying joint slows to a trickle.

Catch and contain

Put a bucket under the leak, towels around it, and move anything electrical away from the wet floor.

Leave a shower wall alone

If water seeps from behind a tiled wall, close the main and do not break the tile. We find the valve location first.

Which faucet do you have? How each one fails

The brand on the handle matters less than the internal design. Naming the type helps us bring the right part on the first trip.

Cartridge, single or double handleO-rings dry out and the cartridge scoresReplace the cartridge
Ceramic disc, smooth quarter-turn feelMineral grit chips the discsNew disc cylinder
Tub or shower pressure-balance valveCartridge or seat fails, handle drips or will not shutReplace the cartridge or the whole valve

Burst supply lines and stops that will not turn

The parts under a sink fail more often than the faucet above it. Most sudden floods trace back to something in this list.

Braided stainless supply line

The stainless jacket hides a rubber liner. When the liner splits or the nut loosens, water sprays under the cabinet.

Seized stop valve

Old multi-turn stops can be frozen for years. Forcing one risks snapping the stem, so we replace the stop with a quarter-turn valve.

Corroded compression nut

Green or white crust at a joint means a slow leak. It can fail all at once.

Flexible copper riser

Thin tubing kinks and cracks where it bends behind the cabinet.

Loose spout or handle seal

A leak at the faucet body soaks the countertop and drips under it, which looks like a supply problem.

What to tell the dispatcher

Thirty seconds of detail saves a second trip.

  • Which fixture: kitchen, bath, tub, shower or outdoor
  • Whether the water leaks at the handle, the spout, or underneath
  • Hot, cold, or both
  • Whether the stops turn and whether the main shutoff is closed
  • A photo of the valve or the cabinet, if you can send one

Shower valve and tub faucet leaks

A shower valve lives inside the wall, and a tub spout and diverter hang off the same plumbing. When the valve cartridge fails, the handle drips, the shower will not shut off, or the temperature swings. Some tub walls have a rear access panel, and many do not.

That is where an honest limit comes in. If the wall has to be opened, the plumbing repair is ours and the tile or surround repair is a finishing trade. We tell you which one applies before work starts, so there is no surprise cost on the second day.

A shower or tub that fills with water instead of draining is a separate issue. A hair-and-soap clog at the trap is the usual cause, and our drain cleaning service clears it.

Replacing fixtures and what the Dolton water report says about lead

Village of Dolton water comes from Chicago's Lake Michigan supply. The 2025 report lists lead at 10.7 ppb at the 90th percentile, under the 15 ppb action level, with 3 sampled sites over it. The report says lead in tap water comes mainly from service lines and home plumbing.

That is why a fixture swap is a good moment to ask questions. A faucet tells us nothing about the line that feeds the house. Ask the Dolton Water Department at 708-201-3293 what material your service line is, and test your tap if you are concerned. We do not call the water safe or unsafe.

Licensing and what we recommend a licensed plumber handles

Illinois licenses plumbers under the Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320), and the state publishes the Illinois Plumbing Code as 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890. The code is the standard the work is judged against. Swapping a cartridge or a washer is simple work. Opening a wall to reach a shutoff, or replacing a valve body that is soldered in, is the point where we recommend a licensed plumber.

If a company quotes a faucet repair, you can check its state license in a minute with the IDPH License Verification tool.
