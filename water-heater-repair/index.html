Water heater repair in Dolton, IL means finding the one failed part that is keeping a tank from heating or holding water, and fixing it if the tank itself is still sound. Our 24/7 emergency line is answered nights, weekends and holidays, because a heater that quits at 6 a.m. in January cannot wait for office hours.

If water is spreading, close the cold supply valve on top of the tank first. If you smell gas, skip everything else and follow the gas steps below. Then call our Dolton dispatch line and tell us what you see.

No hot water or a leaking heater right now

Do these in order. None needs tools beyond your hands and, for the drain step, a garden hose.

Close the cold water valve

It sits on the pipe entering the top of the tank. Turn the handle until it stops. A tank that cannot refill cannot keep leaking for long.

Cut the fuel

On an electric unit, switch off its breaker so the elements are not energized in a half-empty tank. On a gas unit, turn the gas control knob to OFF or PILOT.

Leave hot water alone

Water in the tank can run well above 120 degrees. Do not open the drain valve while it is hot, and keep children away from the puddle.

Move what the water can reach

Lift boxes, cardboard and a dehumidifier off the floor. If water is near an outlet or the panel, stay out and call us from upstairs.

Note the leak location

Top, side, bottom, or a drip from the small pipe beside the tank. That detail tells us whether we are bringing a part or a new unit.

If you smell gas near the heater, leave first

A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater is not a repair call yet. Do not look for the source, and do not flip switches or use your phone inside. Walk everyone outside, then call Nicor Gas at 888-642-6748 or 911, and stay away until they say it is safe.

Call us after the gas utility has cleared the house. We never work on a gas connection while there is an active smell, and we cannot promise how fast any utility arrives, so do not wait indoors for anyone.

Why there is no hot water

Each fuel type fails in its own way. Here is what we test first.

Pilot will not stay lit

On a standing-pilot gas unit, a dirty pilot tube or a weak thermocouple lets the flame die. Both are small parts.

Gas control valve fault

The valve is the thermostat and safety shutoff in one body. When it fails, we explain it and replace it rather than coaxing it along. Never open one yourself.

Burned-out electric element

Electric tanks have an upper and a lower element. One dead element gives lukewarm water or short hot showers. We test each with a meter.

Tripped high-limit switch

A red reset button on an electric unit trips when the water overheats. If it trips again after you press it, something is failing and it needs a visit.

Sediment on the tank floor

Hard minerals settle and insulate the burner or cover the lower element. Dolton hardness is not published, so test your tap before assuming anything.

Dip tube or mixing problem

A broken cold-water dip tube sends cold water to the outlet, so the hot supply runs out fast even though the tank is hot.

The temperature and pressure relief valve is a safety part

That brass valve on the side of the tank with a pipe running down is the temperature and pressure relief valve, or T&P valve. It opens if the tank gets too hot or too pressurized. If it drips or spits, do not cap it, plug it or screw the discharge pipe shut.

A valve that drips every few days can mean pressure from a closed system, a missing expansion tank, or a heater overheating. Replacing the valve without asking why it opened only resets the problem. We check house pressure with a gauge on a hose bib and report the number.

Repair it or replace it

A repair makes sense when the tank is sound. Here is the honest split.

Repair makes sense when

  • The leak is at a fitting, valve or drain, not the shell
  • The failed part is an element, thermocouple, thermostat or relief valve
  • The tank is young and has a clean inside
  • The fix costs far less than a new unit

Replace instead when

  • Water seeps from the tank body or its base
  • The heater is over 10 to 12 years old and has needed several repairs
  • Rust-colored hot water points to a corroded interior
  • A control valve plus a second failure costs close to a new unit

After a basement flood, do not relight the heater

Cook County's hazard mitigation plan describes repeated basement flooding in parts of Dolton. If water reached your heater's burner area or its controls, do not relight a pilot or flip the breaker back on. Wet controls can fail in ways you cannot see.

Call us to inspect the unit first. Floodwater also brings sewage and silt, so we also check what else in the basement needs attention. See sump pump repair if the basement keeps taking on water. We also work in Harvey, a few miles west.

Safety guidelines that apply to water heater repair

A water heater mixes water, gas or high-amperage electricity and a pressure relief valve, so a few guidelines are not optional. Nicor Gas guidance says to leave the building and call 888-642-6748 or 911 if you smell gas, and the relief valve discharge should never be capped or plugged.

The Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, covers the water connections, and the manufacturer's manual covers the gas and electrical parts. We follow both, and we tell you when the age of the tank makes a repair poor value.

Sources: Nicor Gas customer guidance, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890, manufacturer manuals.
