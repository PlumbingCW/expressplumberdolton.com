Floods that shaped the village

South Holland's worst floods came in 1957, 1981 and 1990. In 1990 the Little Calumet River crested 21 feet above normal and took four days to subside. The village responded by adopting a flood-proofing rebate program in 1994 that offered owner-occupants 25 percent of project costs, with a cap, for overhead sewers, backflow valves, drain tile and foundation work.

A 2016 pilot for sewer backups covers half the cost, also with a cap. These figures come from a 2019 Pew Charitable Trusts brief, so treat them as history. Call South Holland village hall at 708-339-2323 and ask whether the program is current before you plan a job around it.

Source: Pew Charitable Trusts brief on South Holland flood-proofing, 2019, citing the village.

The jobs those floods point to

A rebate program that covers these items tells you which jobs the village sees as flood protection.

Sump pump

Moves groundwater out of the pit. If yours is old, noisy or silent in a storm, see sump pump repair.

Overhead sewer or backflow valve

Keeps sewage from rising into floor drains when the public line backs up. Often a new install rather than a repair.

Drain tile and downspouts

Water directed away from the foundation lowers the load on the pump. We say honestly when this is a landscaping fix, not a plumbing one.

Foundation sealing

Cracks that weep after rain. Our basement waterproofing page explains what sealing does and what it does not.

When the sewer line is the problem

Water that comes back up a floor drain during a storm can mean a cracked or root-packed line under the yard. We check with a camera, and the footage tells us whether a cleaning will hold or whether sewer line repair is the real fix.

A line that fails again and again is a repair question, not a cleaning one. We say so before we run a cable a third time.

Repair the pump or add backup protection?

Most South Holland basements need one of two things, and sometimes both.

Fix or replace the pump

  • Solves a pump that will not start or run
  • Usually a same-day appointment when available
  • Does nothing for a backing-up floor drain
  • Best when the rest of the system is sound

Add a backflow valve or overhead sewer

  • Keeps sewage out of floor drains in a storm
  • A planned install, not an emergency fix
  • Costs more up front, so ask about rebates
  • Best after repeated sewer backups

If you are not sure which fits your basement, a technician can look at the pit, the floor drain and the line in a single visit.

Questions to ask before you plan a flood-proofing job

A few answers will save you a wasted trip to village hall.

  • Is the rebate program current, and does my property qualify
  • Do I own the home and live in it, or rent
  • Does the permit office need drawings before work starts
  • Do I have sewer backup coverage on my insurance
  • Which fix matches what actually flooded my basement

Codes and permits for backwater valves and overhead sewers

A backwater valve or an overhead sewer is a plumbing installation, so it falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890. According to the village's own published rebate rules, as reported in 2019, such work was eligible for a partial rebate for owner-occupants, and the village hall can tell you whether that program is current.

Permit rules vary, so ask South Holland's village hall before you hire anyone. The village number for water questions is 708-339-2323, and you can confirm a plumber's state license with the IDPH License Verification tool.
