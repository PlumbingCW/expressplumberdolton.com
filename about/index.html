Who we are

Dolton Express Plumbing is a plumbing service line at 1205 E Sibley Blvd in Dolton, Illinois. We take calls around the clock from homeowners, landlords and small businesses in Dolton, Harvey, Riverdale, South Holland and Calumet City. Calls to our line may be recorded for quality and training purposes.

We do not put names, years in business or job counts on this site, because we would rather show you how we work than ask you to take a number on faith.

How we work

A dispatcher listens first. We ask where the water is, whether you can reach the main valve, and whether anything electrical is wet, and we tell you what to do while you wait. Then we book the first available appointment for your address, with same-day appointments when available.

On site, the plumber finds the cause, explains it in plain words, and gives you a clear price before work starts. You decide. If a repair is not worth the money, such as a very old water heater with a leaking tank, we say that too.

How to check any plumber, including us

Illinois licenses plumbers through the Illinois Department of Public Health under the Illinois Plumbing License Law. IDPH runs a public License Verification tool, and plumbing contractors must be registered with the state and carry liability and workers' compensation insurance. Ask for the name a plumber works under, look it up, and call the Dolton Housing and Permits office at 708-201-3261 if you are unsure whether your job needs a permit.

What a visit looks like

When the plumber arrives, expect a short conversation before any tool comes out. They confirm where the water is coming from, shut off what needs shutting off, and look at the problem. Then they tell you what they found, what it takes to fix it and what it costs, before work starts.

Afterward, they test the repair, explain what changed and tell you what to watch for. If the same problem is likely to return, such as a drain with tree roots in it, they say so and explain your options, including the ones that cost more now and less later.

Where our local knowledge comes from

We build our local pages from public sources, not guesses. The Village of Dolton publishes its water quality report, which shows the village buys Lake Michigan water from the City of Chicago. Cook County's hazard mitigation plan describes where Dolton basements flood repeatedly. The Illinois Department of Insurance explains what homeowners policies usually leave out, such as sewer backup. We cite what they say and leave out what we cannot confirm.

That is also why you will not find a made-up hardness number or an invented permit rule on this site. If a fact is not published, we tell you who to ask. For Dolton permits, that is the Housing and Permits office, which answers questions about plumbing permit applications.
