Repiping in Dolton, IL means replacing the water supply pipes inside a house, in part or in full, because the old ones are leaking, corroded or no longer delivering enough water. The 24/7 line is answered if you have questions, and same-day appointments are offered when available, but most repipes are planned rather than rushed.

Signs your pipes may be near the end

One sign alone is not enough. Several together are a reason to get an assessment.

  • Rust-colored or brown water when a tap has sat unused
  • Low pressure on several fixtures, with clean aerators
  • Repeated leaks in different spots, not the same one
  • Green or white crust on copper, or flaking on galvanized steel
  • Pinhole drips that appear at a joint or along a run
  • Pipe visible in a basement that looks pitted or swollen

Full repipe or fix as it fails

Neither is wrong for everyone. It depends on how much pipe is failing.

Repipe the house

  • Ends repeat leaks in aging pipe
  • Can raise pressure and flow
  • One project, one set of wall openings
  • Larger up-front cost

Repair as needed

  • Lower cost today when only one section is bad
  • Fine for a single isolated leak
  • Each break opens a wall again
  • Does not fix pipes that are all aging

A burst or a pinhole in a single run belongs with our burst pipe repair work first, and a repipe is the follow-up if that single break exposed wider corrosion.

How a repipe works

A typical job follows this order.

1

Walkthrough and plan

We trace the existing lines, note every fixture, and choose a route that disturbs the least wall.

2

Shutoff and access

Water is turned off, and small openings are cut where new pipe must pass. Basements and crawlspaces keep openings to a minimum.

3

New pipe and fittings

New supply lines connect the main, the water heater and each fixture. Old lines are cut out or abandoned in the wall, whichever is safer.

4

Pressure test and restore water

We test before anything is closed up, then turn the water back on and check each fixture.

What the work disturbs

Expect the water off for part of a day or more, depending on the house. Drywall openings are patched at a basic level, but painting and finish carpentry are separate trades. Homes with open basement joists are faster, while homes with finished ceilings take longer.

We cover floors, keep a path clear and tell you when each fixture is back in use. If a leak has already wet a wall, the repair of that damage belongs with a restoration company, since we repair pipes and do not do water damage restoration.

When a repipe is not the right answer

Low pressure does not always mean bad pipes. A partly closed valve, a failing pressure reducing valve or a leak in the buried service line can all produce the same symptom, and our leak detection work rules those out first. If the service line is the problem, the relevant page is water line repair.

Discolored water is another trap. It can come from pipes inside the house or from the water main, so ask the Dolton Water Department at 708-201-3293 whether work on the main has stirred sediment. If it clears after a few minutes, a repipe may not be needed.

What to do before a repipe starts

A repipe opens walls and shuts water off for part of a day. Doing these things first keeps the visit smooth and the house livable.

  • Move furniture, art and fragile items away from walls and ceilings where pipes run
  • Plan where the household will wash, drink and cook while the water is off
  • Locate the main shutoff and meter so the plumber can confirm the plan
  • Ask the Dolton Housing and Permits office whether a permit applies, because rules vary
  • Photograph the walls and ceilings first so you have a record of their condition
