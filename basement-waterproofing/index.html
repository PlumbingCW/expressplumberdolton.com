Basement waterproofing in Dolton, IL, from a plumber's side, means keeping out the water that arrives through pipes and drains. That covers floor drains, sewer connections, sump pits and underground downspout lines. Our 24/7 line is answered, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Most basements are wet for one of three reasons. Sewage or storm water comes back up a drain, groundwater pushes in at the floor edge, or rain pours off the roof and soaks the soil by the wall. Only the first of those is mainly plumbing, and the right fix depends on identifying which one you have.

What plumbing does not fix

We will not seal a foundation crack, regrade your yard or paint the walls. Those are other trades, and we say so before you hire anyone. A coat of sealer on a basement floor does not stop water that is pushing up from below or backing in through a drain.

What we can do is test the plumbing paths, trace where water is entering, and tell you which fix is ours. If you need a masonry or grading contractor, we will say so and describe what we found so you can brief them.

What a plumber can add to a basement

These are the plumbing measures that actually change how a basement handles water.

Backwater valve

A check valve in the building drain that closes when sewage flows the wrong way. It needs an accessible cleanout and a yearly look to make sure the flap moves freely.

Overhead sewer

Basement fixtures drain into a sealed pit and are pumped up above the sewer line. Waste cannot flow back down into the house.

Floor drain check

We run water, inspect the trap and look at the pipe with a camera. A dry trap lets sewer gas in, and a clogged drain fails when you need it.

Interior drain and sump tie-in

A drain channel at the wall base feeds a sump pit. We handle the plumbing, the pit and the pump. Concrete work may involve another contractor.

Downspout tracing

We find where underground downspout lines end. Whether a downspout may connect to a sewer is a village question, so ask before you rely on it.

Backwater valve or overhead sewer?

Both address sewer backup, but they work in different ways and suit different houses.

Backwater valve

  • Installed in the building drain, so there is less work than a pit and pump
  • Closes automatically when sewage flows backward
  • Basement fixtures cannot drain while it is closed, so you stop using them
  • Needs periodic cleaning and a clear access lid

Overhead sewer

  • Pump lifts waste above the street sewer level
  • Basement toilets and drains can keep working during a backup
  • Needs electricity, a sealed pit and a pump that must be maintained
  • A bigger project that reworks the basement drain layout

If you also have a pit pump to maintain, our sump pump repair page covers the pump itself, which we keep separate from this page.

Dolton's flood record and why it changes the plan

Cook County's hazard mitigation plan for Dolton describes urban and repetitive basement flooding across whole neighborhoods. It lists federal and state disaster declarations covering Dolton-area flooding, including April 2013 and storms in July 2010 and August 2007.

The plan also proposes retrofitting about 500 homes with overhead sewer systems in areas where flooding happens when the sewer backs up in severe rain. These are proposals, not finished work. The practical point is the same for any house: if your basement floods mainly when the sewer is overloaded, a backwater valve or overhead sewer addresses it, and more pumping does not.

What to note before we visit

A short log makes the visit more useful.

  • Dates and times of each wet event, and whether it was raining
  • Which drain, wall or corner shows water first
  • Photos of the water line and anything it reached
  • Whether the toilet or washer was in use when it started
  • Your insurance policy wording on sewer backup and sump pump coverage
