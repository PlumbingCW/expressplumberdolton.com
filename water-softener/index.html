We start with a question most people skip: what is in your water? The Village of Dolton's water quality report does not list hardness, so we cannot give you a number. We recommend testing your tap.

Signs hard water may be the cause

None of these proves hard water. Together they are a reason to test.

  • White crusty scale on faucets and showerheads
  • Spots on glasses and shower doors after drying
  • Soap that does not lather and leaves a film
  • Weak hot water flow from scale in pipes or the heater
  • A water heater that rumbles from sediment

Salt-based softener or salt-free conditioner

They do different jobs. One removes hardness minerals. The other changes how scale forms.

Salt-based softener

  • Swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium
  • Actually reduces scale on fixtures and in the heater
  • Needs salt refills and a drain for the regeneration water
  • Adds some sodium or potassium to the water

Salt-free conditioner

  • Changes how scale crystals form, so they stick less
  • No salt and no regeneration drain
  • Does not lower the measured hardness
  • Results vary, so set modest expectations

Our water heater installation page covers heaters, which are often where scale shows first, and we serve South Holland homes with the same systems.

Filtration is a separate question

Softeners handle hardness. Filters handle other things. Do not confuse them.

Sediment filter

Catches sand and rust flakes. It protects fixtures and other filters downstream.

Carbon filter

Reduces chlorine taste and odor. Chlorine in Dolton's 2025 report was 1.0 to 1.4 ppm.

Under-sink filter

Treats one faucet. It is a smaller project than a whole-house system.

Reverse osmosis

Treats drinking water at one tap and wastes some water. It suits specific goals.

What a softener does not remove: lead

A water softener does not remove lead. The Village of Dolton's 2025 water quality report lists a lead 90th percentile of 10.7 ppb against a 15 ppb action level, with 3 sampled sites above that level. It also lists copper at 0.0335 ppm, with none over its 1.3 ppm action level.

The report says lead in drinking water comes mainly from service lines and home plumbing, and that the village has developed a service line material inventory. We do not say your water is safe or unsafe. Ask the Dolton Water Department at 708-201-3293 or Public Works at 708-201-3280 what material your service line is.

Filters that address lead are a separate topic with their own labeling, so ask before you assume a filter does it. Our water heater repair and leak detection pages cover plumbing problems that scale can hide.

Source: Village of Dolton Consumer Confidence Report, 2025.

Fixing a softener that stopped working

Common softener failures are simple. A salt bridge forms a crust over the water in the brine tank. The resin bed wears out after years of service. A stuck control valve fails to regenerate, or the injector clogs. Hard water in the shower after you have a softener means one of these has happened.

We check the bypass valve, the brine tank, the drain line and the control head. If the resin is spent, we tell you so and price a replacement against a new unit. A repair is not always worth it on an old system.
