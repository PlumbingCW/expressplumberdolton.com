Outdoor Water Faucet Repair in Dolton, IL

Most outdoor faucet leaks come from a worn washer or a loose packing nut, and both are small repairs. Here is how to tell which one you have, and where DIY ends.

An outdoor water faucet, also called a hose bib or sillcock, is a valve on the outside wall of your house that feeds a garden hose. Outdoor water faucet repair in Dolton, IL usually means one of three jobs: replacing a washer that drips from the spout, tightening or replacing the packing that leaks behind the handle, or swapping a faucet that split over winter.

The fix depends on where the water shows up. Water at the spout is a different problem from water at the handle, and water inside the wall is a third problem that this article will not talk you into fixing yourself.

Where the water shows up tells you the repair

Check the faucet with the hose removed and the handle fully closed. Then match what you see to the table.

How to fix a dripping hose bib

You need an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver, a few replacement washers and a rag. Allow about 30 minutes.

1

Shut off the supply

Close the interior shut-off valve for the outside line if you have one. If not, turn off the main water valve, then open the faucet to drain it.

2

Remove the handle and packing nut

Take out the handle screw, lift the handle off, and loosen the packing nut with a wrench. Turn it counterclockwise and keep the stem straight as it comes out.

3

Replace the washer or packing

A flat rubber washer sits on the end of the stem, held by a small brass screw. Swap it for one of the same size, and wrap new graphite packing string around the stem under the nut.

4

Reassemble and test

Thread the stem back in, snug the packing nut until the handle still turns freely, then restore the water. Check the spout and the handle for drips for a few minutes.

If the whole faucet needs replacing, you will choose between two designs. The difference is where the shut-off seat sits.

Standard hose bib

  • Seat sits right behind the handle, close to the outer wall
  • Cheaper and simple to swap
  • Holds water in the barrel if a hose stays attached
  • Needs a separate interior shut-off to drain for winter

Frost-free sillcock

  • Long stem puts the seat deep inside the heated space
  • Costs more up front but drains itself when shut
  • Must tilt slightly downward so water runs out
  • Still splits if a hose is left on in a freeze

Neither design survives a hose left screwed on through a cold snap. A frost-free faucet only helps when the barrel can empty, which is why we also check the tilt when we do faucet repair outdoors.

The practical step is simple. Remove and drain hoses before the first hard freeze, shut the inside valve if your faucet has one, and open the outside spout to let the line empty. Fall shut-down checklist for outdoor faucets

Run through this list once before the first freeze. It takes ten minutes and avoids most winter faucet damage.

  • Unscrew every garden hose and splitter, including the ones on a timer
  • Drain the hoses and store them indoors
  • Close the interior shut-off valve to each outside faucet, if one exists
  • Open the outdoor spout and leave it open so the barrel can empty
  • Look for a faucet that tilts upward, which can trap water
  • Cover a standard hose bib with an insulated dome only after it is drained

A faucet that froze and cracked in winter

A split faucet often shows no sign until spring. You open the spout, and water sprays out of the side of the barrel or soaks the basement ceiling. The crack is usually in the brass, or in the copper right behind it, and a new washer will not touch it.

If you turn on the outside water and see wet drywall or hear a hiss in the wall, shut the water off at the main valve right away. For that situation read our page on burst pipe repair, because water inside a wall keeps running until someone stops it.

When to stop and call a plumber

Replacing a washer is a safe weekend job. Cutting out and sweating a new copper fitting is not, because it needs a torch near framing and a tested connection. Call a plumber if the faucet is soldered into the wall and the stem will not come out, if the shut-off valve inside will not close, or if you see water stains on the ceiling below.

Also stop if you cannot find the cause. Repeated drips can come from a seat that is pitted, which a new washer cannot seal. In that case the whole faucet should be replaced. Illinois requires plumbing work to follow the Illinois Plumbing Code, and permit rules vary, so ask the Dolton building department before cutting into pipe.

What a hose bib repair usually does not fix

A new washer stops a drip, and nothing else. It will not fix low pressure, which usually comes from a partly closed valve or a kinked hose. It will not fix a cracked pipe inside a wall. It also will not fix a vacuum breaker that sprays because it is blocked or worn.

If you have a brand new faucet and still see a drip, check that the stem washer is seated flat and that the packing nut is tight but not crushed. Overtightening is a common mistake and can score the stem. A little graphite packing and a gentle quarter turn usually does it.

What to do when an outdoor faucet leaks or freezes

Outdoor faucets fail in two ways, a slow drip at the handle and a split pipe behind the wall. This short list covers what to do in both cases, and it follows the standard guidance to stop the water first.

  • Shut off the interior valve that feeds the outdoor faucet if you can find it
  • Disconnect the hose, because a hose left on traps water and freezes the line
  • If water sprays inside the wall or basement, close the main valve and call the 24/7 line
  • Do not use a torch to thaw a frozen line near walls or insulation
  • Take a photo of any damage before you repair or dry anything
