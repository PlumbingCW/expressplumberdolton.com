Choosing between gas, electric and hybrid

Each type has real trade-offs. Which one fits depends on what your house already has.

Natural gas tank

Heats quickly and recovers fast, so long showers back to back are easier. It needs a gas line and a vent that carries combustion gases outdoors.

Standard electric tank

Simple to install where a 240 volt circuit exists. It recovers more slowly than gas, and the running cost depends on your electric rate.

Hybrid heat pump unit

Pulls heat from the surrounding air, so it uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank. It needs open air space, makes a fan noise and drips condensate.

Power-vent gas tank

Uses a fan to push exhaust through PVC piping, which gives more flexibility on where the heater sits. It needs an outlet and makes some noise.

Tankless on demand

Heats water as it flows and needs no storage tank. It has its own page and its own gas or electrical demands. See tankless water heater.

Where the heater goes decides most of the job

The location controls everything else. A gas heater needs combustion air and a clear vent path, a hybrid needs open space around it, and any tank needs a drain, a floor that will not rot, and room to reach the valves. Measuring the clearance before ordering avoids a unit that does not fit.

We check the distance to the vent termination, the slope of the connector, the nearest floor drain, and whether the tank can be reached for a future flush. If the old spot is wrong, we say so and show an alternate.

Decide these before the install visit

You do not need all the answers. These are the ones that save time.

  • Gas, electric or hybrid, and why you lean that way
  • Tank size, based on household size and bathroom count
  • Whether the current vent pipe is rusted or undersized
  • Where the old unit sits and whether you would rather move it
  • Whether you want a drain pan and a leak alarm under the tank
  • Whether anyone needs hot water during the install

What an install visit looks like

A normal visit moves through the same four stages.

1

1. Walk the space

We look at the old unit, vent, gas line or circuit, and the drain, then confirm the plan and the price with you.

2

2. Drain and remove

We shut off water and fuel, drain the old tank, disconnect it and carry it out.

3

3. Set and connect

The new heater goes in with a relief valve and discharge pipe, new flexible connectors, a gas shutoff or circuit connection, and the vent.

4

4. Test and explain

We fill the tank, purge air, check for leaks, test combustion draft on gas units, set the temperature and show you the shutoff and the label.

Repairing hybrid, electric and gas units

Short version: yes, we repair all three, but the limits differ. Electric tanks usually need an element or a thermostat. Gas tanks usually need a thermocouple, burner cleaning or a gas control valve. Hybrid units add a compressor, a fan and a condensate line, and parts availability depends on the brand. For a heater that is down now, see water heater repair.
