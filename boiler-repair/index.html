Boiler repair in Dolton, IL covers the water side of a heating system: leaks, pressure problems, circulator pumps, valves, expansion tanks and the piping to radiators or baseboard. Our 24/7 line is answered, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. We work on hot-water and steam systems.

A hot-water boiler heats water and pumps it through the house in a loop. A steam boiler turns water into steam that rises to radiators, then falls back as condensate. They fail differently, so the first question we ask is which one you have.

Carbon monoxide and gas smells come first

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness or nausea that eases when they leave the house, get everyone outside and call 911. Do not stop to open windows or check the boiler. Call us after the building is safe and the responders say so.

If you smell natural gas, do not look for the source. Leave right away, avoid switches, phones and vehicles until you are outside, then call Nicor Gas at 888-642-6748 or 911. That call comes before the plumber, every time.

Problems we see most often

Most boiler calls fall into a handful of patterns.

No heat

Often a tripped switch, a thermostat that is off or out of power, a failed circulator or a closed zone valve. A boiler that is not firing at all may be a heating specialist's call.

Pressure that drops

Many home hot-water boilers sit around 12 to 15 psi when cold, but your boiler's label rules. A steady drop means water is leaking somewhere, or the expansion tank has failed.

Pressure that climbs

A waterlogged expansion tank or a fill valve that leaks through pushes pressure up until the relief valve opens.

Leaks

Drips at the relief valve, circulator flanges, radiator valves or the boiler body each point to a different repair.

Noises

Banging in steam pipes usually means water is trapped in a sagging line. Kettle sounds can mean sediment in the boiler.

Cold radiators

Air trapped at the top of a radiator or a pitch problem in the piping. Bleeding air is often the first fix.

What an annual service visit looks like

Yearly service is not exciting, but it catches the small leak that becomes the big one.

1

Look at the whole system

We inspect the boiler, piping and radiators for drips, stains and corrosion.

2

Check the water side

Pressure or sight glass level, the relief valve, the expansion tank and the fill valve are tested.

3

Test pumps and valves

Circulators, zone valves and radiator valves are checked for flow and for seeps.

4

Report and hand off

We tell you what we found. Burner cleaning and combustion testing go to a heating specialist, and we say so.

Check this before you call about no heat

These checks take a few minutes and sometimes solve it.

  • The thermostat is set to heat, and its batteries are fresh
  • The boiler's power switch is on and the breaker has not tripped
  • The pressure gauge or sight glass reads in its normal range
  • No puddle sits under the boiler or around the relief valve
  • Note any blinking light or code, then take a photo of it
  • If you smell gas, stop and leave, do not troubleshoot

Standards and safety rules that touch boiler work

Boilers sit next to gas, combustion air and venting, so the safety rules matter more here than on most plumbing jobs. Nicor Gas customer guidance says a gas smell means leave the building at once and call 888-642-6748 or 911 before anything else, and we follow the same order of operations on every boiler call.

The water side of a boiler falls under the Illinois Plumbing Code, 77 Ill. Adm. Code 890. Permit rules vary, so ask the Dolton Housing and Permits office at 708-201-3261 before a replacement and keep the manufacturer's manual for the model on site.
