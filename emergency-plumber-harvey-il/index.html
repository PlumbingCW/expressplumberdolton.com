Three problems that fill most Harvey calls

Most plumbing emergencies fit one of a few patterns. Find yours below and we will start from there.

Drain that will not clear

A kitchen sink, tub or floor drain that stays full after a plunger. We run a cable, then a camera if the water level does not drop. See our drain cleaning work for how the tools differ.

Sewage smell or wet basement floor

A main line blockage sends water to the lowest opening in the house, usually a basement floor drain. A cracked or root-filled line needs sewer line repair, not just another cable pass.

No hot water, or a leaking tank

A pilot that will not stay lit, a tripped element or a puddle under the tank. Water heater repair often fixes a failed thermostat or valve the same day. A rusted-through tank needs replacing.

Pipe leaking inside a wall

A stain spreading on drywall, or a meter that moves with every tap closed. We trace the leak before opening any wall, which keeps the repair small.

Before We Arrive: what to do at your Harvey home

Five things you can do in the next few minutes, none of which needs tools.

Shut off the main water valve

It is usually near the water meter, often in the basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops if a pipe is leaking or a tank has failed.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, a furnace or the panel, stay out and switch off the breaker only if you can reach it dry. Otherwise call us and the electric utility.

Stop using drains

No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher while a line is blocked. Every gallon you add has to go somewhere.

Clear the area

Lift rugs, boxes and cardboard off a wet floor and keep children and pets out of sewage water.

Call the 24/7 line

Say which fixtures are affected and your ZIP. If you smell gas, leave first and call Nicor Gas at 888-642-6748 or 911 before you call us.

What we know about Harvey and what we do not

Harvey is a city in Cook County, ZIP 60426, founded in the early 1890s. Halsted Street, Illinois Route 1, runs along its east side, and two Metra Electric stations and the Pace Harvey Transportation Center sit in town. Harvey is also one of the communities served by the Thornton Composite Reservoir, which takes in combined sewer overflow during large storms.

None of that tells us what pipe is in your house. A city founded in the 1890s has plenty of older building stock, but your home could be any age, so we ask the year built and look at the pipe on site rather than guess. The reservoir does not stop a private floor drain from backing up, so a basement that floods still needs a plumber.

Older pipe, Halsted Street traffic and other practical limits

A plumber working along Halsted or in the blocks near the Metra stations still needs a clear spot to park and a path to the main valve. If you rent, tell us where the shutoff and cleanout are or who holds the key, and we note it on the work order.

We will also say when a repair is not worth it. A water heater with a rusted-through tank is a replacement job, not a repair, and a line that has failed twice may need to be dug rather than cleaned again. You hear that before the work, not after.
