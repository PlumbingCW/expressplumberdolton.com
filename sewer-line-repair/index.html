Sewer line repair in Dolton, IL means fixing the buried pipe that carries waste from your house to the village sewer. When that pipe cracks, collapses, separates or gets pushed apart by roots, drains slow, floors flood and sewer gas can seep in. Our 24/7 emergency line is answered nights and weekends.

If sewage is on the floor, stop using water and keep people away from it. The next section covers the first steps, and then we will walk through how a repair is chosen.

Sewage in the house: first steps before we arrive

A broken sewer line is a sanitation problem as well as a plumbing one.

Shut off water use

No flushing, no laundry and no showers. Water sent into a broken line ends up on your floor.

Turn off power to wet areas

If sewage reaches an outlet, a furnace or a panel, do not step into it. Call the 24/7 line and, if you feel unsafe, call 911.

Keep children and pets away

Sewage carries bacteria. Wear boots and gloves if you must cross the area, and wash well afterward.

Ventilate if you smell gas

A rotten egg smell can be sewer gas, but if you think it is natural gas, leave the building and call Nicor Gas at 888-642-6748 or 911 first.

Take photos

Record the damage for your insurance claim before cleanup begins.

How a sewer line fails

Most failures fall into a handful of patterns. A camera tells us which one you have.

Cracked or broken pipe

Clay, cast iron and old plastic can split from ground movement, heavy loads or age. Waste leaks out and soil washes in.

Root intrusion

Roots find the tiny gap at a joint, grow into a mat and catch paper and grease until the line is blocked.

Offset or separated joint

Two pipe sections slide out of line. Waste catches on the lip and builds into a stoppage.

Belly or sag

A low spot holds water and solids. It slowly clogs and may need a section replaced.

Collapse

A crushed section stops flow completely. A cable will not pass it, and digging is usually needed.

Trenchless lining or dig and replace?

Both are real repairs. Which one fits depends on the pipe, the depth and what sits above it.

Trenchless lining

  • Little digging, so lawns and driveways stay in place
  • Works on a pipe that is cracked but still round
  • Does not fix a collapsed or badly offset section
  • Slightly reduces the inside diameter

Dig and replace

  • New pipe fixes a collapse, sag or major offset
  • Lets us correct the slope along the run
  • Needs excavation, restoration and a longer job
  • Requires JULIE 811 locates before any digging

A camera inspection tells us which one fits. We tell you plainly if the camera shows a pipe too damaged for it.

Which part of the line is yours

Many homeowners are surprised to learn that they own the sewer pipe from the house to the point where it connects to the larger line, and that a village or utility may own the rest. Where that handoff sits depends on the local rules and the property, so we do not guess.

Before any dig, ask Dolton Public Works at 708-201-3280 who owns which part of the line at your address. After hours, the village directs callers to its Dispatch Center at 708-201-3240. That answer decides whether a repair is your bill or the village's to handle.

When a repair is not the answer

Not every backup means a broken line. A grease blockage, a heavy storm or a village main under pressure can all push sewage into a basement. If the line is sound, sewer line cleaning or a sump pump check may solve the problem for less.
