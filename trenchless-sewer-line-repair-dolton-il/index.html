Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Dolton, IL: Lining vs Digging

Trenchless repair can save a lawn and driveway, but it is not right for every broken pipe. Here are the methods, the limits and the questions to ask before you agree to either.

Trenchless sewer line repair is a way to fix or replace the underground pipe between your house and the sewer connection without digging a trench along its whole length. A camera inspection comes first. Then the crew works through the cleanout or a small access pit and relines the old pipe or pulls a new one through it. In Dolton, IL, a driveway or front yard often sits on top of the line, so that can matter.

It is not magic. The method has to match the damage, and some damage still needs a shovel.

Trenchless against open-cut digging

Neither is better in the abstract. The pipe decides.

Trenchless repair

  • Small access holes, so less lawn and pavement damage
  • Often a shorter job on site
  • Needs a pipe that is mostly intact and round
  • Cannot fix a belly or a collapsed section

Open-cut digging

  • Fixes bellies, collapses and badly offset joints
  • You can see the pipe and the soil
  • Cuts through lawn, driveway or landscape
  • Usually needs restoration work afterward

The test is a camera run through the line. Our sewer inspection work gives you video and a clear picture of what the pipe looks like.

How the process unfolds

Whichever method suits your pipe, the sequence is similar.

1

Inspect and locate

A camera shows cracks, roots, offsets or a belly. A locator marks the depth and path above ground.

2

Clean the pipe

Roots and scale are cut or jetted out so the liner or new pipe can pass through.

3

Line or burst

For lining, a resin-soaked liner is pulled in and cured to form a pipe inside the old one. For bursting, a cone pulls new pipe through while breaking the old one outward.

4

Reopen and verify

Service connections are reopened, then a final camera pass confirms the new surface is smooth and watertight.

When trenchless is a poor fit

A trustworthy contractor will tell you when to dig. These are the cases where it matters.

A pipe with a belly

A sagging section holds water. A liner follows the shape and keeps the sag, so waste still settles there.

A collapsed pipe

If the pipe has crushed or the pieces moved apart, there may be no path for a liner or cone.

A connection that must be rebuilt

Badly offset joints and sharp bends can stop the process cold.

A line that is too shallow or too deep

Very deep lines, or places where the pipe slopes the wrong way, may call for excavation.

Multiple heavy repairs

If the pipe fails every few feet, replacing the whole run may be the better value.

Call 811 before anybody digs

Illinois law requires calling JULIE at 811 at least 48 hours before digging so utility lines are marked, and the service is free. That applies to open-cut repair and to the access pit for a trenchless job. A reputable crew makes that call as a matter of routine. Ask to see the ticket number before the machine arrives.

The call tells you where the gas, electric and water lines run. It does not map your private sewer lateral, which is why the camera and locator matter.

Questions to ask any sewer contractor

A short list keeps a sales pitch honest.

  • Can I see the camera video that supports the method you recommend
  • Why is lining or bursting right for this pipe, and why not digging
  • Will you call JULIE 811 and share the ticket number
  • Who handles the permit question, and which village office did you ask
  • What does the warranty cover, and is it in writing
  • Is your plumber licensed, and may I verify it with IDPH

Permits, neighbors and your next step
