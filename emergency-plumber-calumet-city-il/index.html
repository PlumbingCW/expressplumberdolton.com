Spring: rain, thaw and sewer backups

Spring storms test every drain in a house. The Cook County hazard plan annex for Calumet City lists 24 FEMA repetitive-loss properties and a levee break in 2008 at Shirley Avenue. The Superior Stormwater Basin serves about 936 properties and currently discharges into the combined sewer system, and the city plans a sewer-separation project.

The annex also describes a sewer backup rebate program run by the city's Department of Inspectional Services. We do not know the current terms, so call the city before you spend money. In the meantime, a backing-up floor drain needs a camera look, and sewer line repair may be the answer if the line itself is cracked.

Before We Arrive: a Calumet City water emergency

The same few steps apply in any season. Do them while you wait.

Shut off the main water valve

Turn it clockwise if a pipe has burst or a tank is leaking. A frozen pipe that has not yet split can also be isolated this way.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, a furnace or the panel, stay out. Call ComEd at 800-334-7661 if you cannot reach the breaker dry.

Clear the area

Lift cardboard and rugs, and keep children and pets away from sewage water.

Stop using drains

Hold off on laundry and flushing while a line is blocked or floor drain is rising.

Hot weather brings heavy downpours and, with them, floor drains that gurgle. A kitchen sink that drains a little slower each week is often grease building up. Our drain cleaning service starts at the fixture, moves to the cleanout, and brings in a camera if the clog returns.

Fall: the water report and your service line

Calumet City draws all its water from Lake Michigan, 20 percent supplied by Chicago and 80 percent by Hammond. The 2024 report lists a lead 90th percentile of 17 ppb, sampled August 26, 2024, against an action level of 15 ppb, with 8 sites over. The report points to the city website for its service line material inventory.

We do not call the water safe or unsafe. We can tell you what pipe is inside your house, and water line repair work can replace old pipe on your side. Ask the Water Department at 708-891-8155 what material your service line is and who is responsible for each piece of it.

Year-round habits that prevent calls

None of these is glamorous. They prevent more emergencies than any gadget.

  • Learn where your main water valve sits and that it turns
  • Keep grease and wipes out of the kitchen drain
  • Test your sump pump before the wet season
  • Insulate pipes in garages and crawlspaces before the first hard freeze
  • Ask your insurance agent about sewer backup coverage
  • Keep the 24/7 number where the whole household can find it

What insurance guidance says about sewer backup in Calumet City

According to the Illinois Department of Insurance, basic homeowners insurance does not cover floods, and standard flood coverage does not typically cover damage from sewer backups or sump pump problems. It advises asking your agent about adding sewer-backup or sump pump coverage, and photographing and inventorying your belongings.

That guidance matters in a city whose hazard plan lists a sewer backup rebate program, because a rebate helps pay for the fix but not for the damage. Call the IDOI consumer line at 1-866-445-5364 if you are unsure what your policy says.

Sources: Illinois Department of Insurance press release, Cook County hazard mitigation plan annex for Calumet City.
